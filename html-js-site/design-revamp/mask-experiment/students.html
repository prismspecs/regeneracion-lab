Study & Research Opportunities

The Regeneración Lab offers opportunities for students to engage in community-based research, decolonial praxis, and collaborative scholarship. We work with undergraduate and graduate students and postdoctoral fellows across disciplines who are committed to research justice and critical-creative methodologies.

Participation Pathways

Ways to Engage

Students can participate through directed research projects, reading groups and study circles, workshops, and collaborative initiatives with our resident scholars. We prioritize hands-on learning that centers community needs and Indigenous and marginalized epistemologies.

  • Directed Research: Supervised research credit in departmental independent study (English, Chicana/o Studies, Environmental Studies).
  • Archival Digitization: Practical preservation work with our Safiya Henderson Holmes Black Arts archive and oral histories.
  • Counter-Mapping: Field and GIS methodologies documenting water rights and ancestral river confluence geography.
  • Graduate Fellowships: Collaborative co-authorship on research justice curriculum and public monographs.
Curricular Assets

Resources & Syllabi

Access our Indigenous Border Studies Syllabus, research justice frameworks, and educational materials developed through our community-based projects. These resources are designed to support critical engagement with decolonial scholarship and praxis.

  • Indigenous Border Studies Syllabus: Multi-thematic open-access curriculum mapping borders, colonial treaties, and carceral geography.
  • Research Justice Toolkits: Consent-based research frameworks, community verification protocols, and ethical citation models.
  • Digital Humanities Codebase: Open-source repository of text analysis and spatial scripts developed in lab workshops.
  • Movement Filmography: Curated primary documentation, recorded seminars, and indigenous water defense media.

Pedagogical Frameworks & Praxis

Our student cohorts, reading groups, and lab collaborations are animated by three core pedagogical commitments designed to dissolve the boundary between academic research and community struggle.

01

Decolonial Epistemologies

Centering Indigenous, Black radical, and Global South traditions of knowledge that predate and resist colonial land partition, water diversion, and linear extraction.

02

Research Justice

Refusing top-down extractive methodologies. Community partners hold veto power, editorial authority, and shared stewardship over archival collections and publications.

03

Critical-Creative Making

Blending rigorous theoretical scholarship with Python data modeling, counter-mapping, poetry archives, and public art to intervene directly in policy and culture.
